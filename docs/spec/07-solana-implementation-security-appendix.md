# Fade Spec 07 — Solana Implementation Security — Appendix

## §5 — The permissionless `settle_ticket` instruction: threat enumeration

Enumerate what a hostile caller — anyone, by design — can influence, then close each:

- **Account substitution.** Already covered exhaustively in §1: `randomness`, `beneficiary`, `vault_usdc`, `payout_escrow`, `integrator` are all pinned by `address =`/`seeds =`/`has_one =` reading from the ticket's or the vault's own already-trusted state, never accepted positionally. Restated here because this is the section where it matters most: the caller supplies 100% of the account list, by definition, so "pin everything that matters" is not optional hygiene here — it's the whole defense.
- **Ordering / transaction composition — the deposit+settle+withdraw bundle.** Because the VRF outcome becomes public and deterministic the instant the randomness account is fulfilled (readable by anyone before they call `settle_ticket`), a searcher can read a pending ticket's outcome off-chain, and if it's a net win *for the vault* (player loses), bundle `deposit → settle_ticket → withdraw` in one atomic transaction to capture a pro-rata share of that specific win with zero prior exposure to the vault's risk — a Solana-native analogue of "just-in-time liquidity" MEV. Defenses, layered:
    - Compute share price only from the internal ledger (§3/§6) — necessary but **not sufficient** on its own, since the attacker doesn't need to manipulate the *price*, only the *timing* of when they hold shares relative to a known-outcome settlement.
    - **Primary defense: 600-second epoch-batched forward pricing with a clean strike** ([Technical Specification](README.md) §2 and §3.8; Spec 01 §3.3, §3.5) — shares are priced only at epoch strikes, and a strike runs only once every ticket opened before the epoch's close has resolved, so a deposit or withdrawal can never buy or sell at a price that leaves a known outcome unapplied. This is what carries the real load, because Solana has no public mempool but a single leader can still sequence multiple transactions from a colluding party within one block — a gap a same-transaction guard alone cannot close.
    - **Secondary, defense-in-depth: use the Instructions sysvar inside `request_deposit` and `request_withdraw` to scan the transaction's top-level instructions and reject any that is not on the allowlist (invariant 13), which excludes a `settle_ticket` or `open_ticket` targeting Fade in the same transaction.** This is the same introspection technique lending protocols use to *require* a repay instruction to exist for flash loans ([Solend flash-loan pattern via instruction sysvar](https://deepwiki.com/solendprotocol/solana-program-library/2.4-flash-loans)) — here used in reverse, to *forbid* composition rather than require it. It is necessary but **not sufficient** on its own — it closes the same-transaction case, not the intra-block, multi-transaction variant that epoch pricing covers; recommend both layers, not one.
- **Compute-budget starvation.** A caller could attach an artificially low `SetComputeUnitLimit` to their own `settle_ticket` transaction, but Solana aborts the *entire* transaction on CU exhaustion with no partial state commit — this cannot corrupt state, only fail that particular caller's attempt. It is a denial-of-service against *that keeper's* transaction landing, not against Fade's invariants. Operationally: keepers should always attach a profiled, generous CU limit and a priority fee rather than relying on defaults (§7 gives the budget numbers to profile against).
- **Front-running the settle.** There is nothing to front-run in the sense of "change the outcome" — the outcome is fixed the instant the VRF result lands on-chain, independent of who calls `settle_ticket` or in what order. The only genuinely exploitable timing is the JIT deposit/withdraw bundle above (already addressed) and racing for any keeper bounty (harmless by design as long as it's fixed/capped, §4).

## §6 — Token handling: rationale and sources

- **Donation attack.** A direct SPL transfer of USDC straight into the vault token account, bypassing `request_deposit` entirely, must be **inert** for share pricing. This is the mechanism behind the well-documented ERC-4626 first-depositor/inflation attack, ported to any share-based vault whose NAV reads a live token balance: attacker deposits a token first-in (getting the only share), donates a large raw amount directly to the vault, inflating `assets/share` so the next legitimate depositor's `amount * total_shares / total_assets` rounds down to zero shares for any deposit smaller than the inflation, and the attacker redeems their one share for everyone's money ([OpenZeppelin: A Novel Defense Against ERC4626 Inflation Attacks](https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/a-novel-defense-against-erc4626-inflation-attacks); [Solodit Checklist: Donation Attacks](https://checkwithhans.substack.com/p/solodit-checklist-explained-3-donation)). **Non-negotiable fix, already stated in §3: `total_assets` MUST be an internal ledger field updated only by `strike_epoch`, `open_ticket` and `settle_ticket`, never derived by reading `token_account.amount` live.** This makes raw donations sit in the vault inertly — harmless, if wasteful — rather than exploitable. An optional, explicitly admin-gated `sweep_donations` instruction can later reconcile stray donated balance into the ledger deliberately, but never automatically or instantly.
- **Token-2022 extensions — restrict V1 to a single, hardcoded, pre-vetted mint (legacy SPL Token program, canonical Circle USDC), and say so plainly:**
    - **Transfer fees** silently shrink the amount actually received vs. requested on every `transfer_checked`, breaking the program's exact-accounting invariants (share math, reservation, payout amounts) unless *every* arithmetic site becomes fee-aware — an enormous, error-prone surface for a program whose entire job is exact solvency.
    - **Transfer hooks** execute arbitrary, mint-controlled CPI logic on every transfer into or out of the vault. Given Fade's threat model already centers on "can the caller substitute a malicious program," an attacker-influenceable transfer hook is functionally a guaranteed CPI hook into every payout — reentrancy, unpredictable side effects, or settlement-freezing failures, for no benefit to V1's actual requirements ([Neodyme: SPL Token-2022 — Don't shoot yourself in the foot with extensions](https://neodyme.io/en/blog/token-2022/); [Chainstack: Transfer Hooks and Fee-on-Transfer](https://chainstack.com/solana-token-2022-fee-transfer-hooks/)).
    - **Confidential transfers** make amounts unreadable on-chain — incompatible with public, verifiable share/liability accounting entirely.
    - **Freeze authority** on an unvetted mint means a third party Fade never chose to trust can freeze the vault's own token account at will. For canonical Circle USDC this is an accepted, known operational reality (Circle can freeze for compliance reasons) that must be *designed for* explicitly (frozen-beneficiary handling, below) rather than an accidental extra risk introduced by accepting an unknown mint.
    - **Decision to record in the spec, not just this checklist:** every relevant account constraint pins `address = config.usdc_mint` on the mint, and explicitly checks the owning token program equals the legacy SPL Token program id (reject Token-2022 outright in V1). Token-2022 support is future work requiring its own dedicated audit pass, not a V1 goal.
- **Frozen-account case — decided (Technical Specification §3.8).** Even with a single vetted mint, an *individual* token account (not the mint) can be frozen by the mint's freeze authority — e.g., Circle freezing a specific holder's USDC for a sanctions/compliance reason. A settlement that could fail would block every later epoch strike, so `settle_ticket` **always succeeds**: it reads the beneficiary account's state *before* any CPI (a failed CPI aborts the transaction, so the branch must be decided by inspection, not by catching an error); if the account is frozen, closed or of the wrong mint, the payout goes to the vault-owned `payout_escrow` and the ticket stays open as `PayoutPending` with the amount recorded; the reservation and the open credit are released in both branches. A permissionless `claim_payout` transfers the escrowed amount to the stored beneficiary — never to another account — whenever it is transferable again, and closes the ticket to `payer`. The vault is never held hostage by one frozen destination.
- **The vault's own token account frozen.** Circle can also freeze `vault_usdc` itself; then no payout, no claim and no withdrawal can move until it is unfrozen, and nothing in the program can route around it. This is the counterparty risk every USDC protocol carries; it is disclosed in the Technical Specification's known limitations and accepted, not engineered around.
- **Correct vault-asset tracking, restated as the section's single rule:** `total_assets`, `total_reserved_liability` and `open_vault_credits` in `Vault`, and the queued deposits and what struck withdrawals still owe in `Queue`, live in an internal ledger updated exactly by `request_deposit`, `strike_epoch`, `claim_withdraw`, `open_ticket`, `settle_ticket` and `expire_ticket`. `spl_token::state::Account::amount` on the vault is **never** read as a source of truth for pricing or availability — only as a reconciliation check that asserts `vault_usdc.amount ≥ total_assets`, `deposit_queue_usdc.amount ≥` the queued deposits and `payable_usdc.amount ≥` what struck withdrawals owe, never the reverse dependency.

## §7 — Denial of service and compute

- **Payout table size.** A fixed-size, **compile-time-bounded** array (`const MAX_OUTCOMES: usize = 32;`), not a `Vec<Outcome>` with dynamic realloc — at ~12–16 bytes/outcome (weight + payout, both `u64`), 32 outcomes is ~512 bytes. Combined with the ticket account's other fixed fields (two pubkeys for integrator/beneficiary, price, nonce, randomness-account pubkey, status enum, reserved-liability figure, rent-payer pubkey, bump, discriminator) the whole account comfortably sits under 1 KB — nowhere near the 10,240-byte BPF serialization cap or the 10 MB per-account maximum, so size is not the binding constraint here; determinism and a compile-time bound on the scan loop are. Reject at `open_ticket` (the only checkpoint, §2) any payout table exceeding `MAX_OUTCOMES` rather than dynamically resizing to fit — resizing reopens realloc-related complexity for no benefit at this scale.
- **Compute budget.** `settle_ticket` is the heaviest instruction (VRF-account deserialization, a bounded ≤32-iteration payout table scan, reservation math, one `transfer_checked` CPI, an account close) and `open_ticket` is comparable (payout table validation loop, three split transfers, prefunding the VRF payer — it makes no oracle call under design B); `strike_epoch` is O(1) by construction (invariant 16) and `request_randomness` is dominated by the bounded `SlotHashes` read and one ORAO CPI. Neither should approach the 200k-CU default; profile both with LiteSVM's compute-unit reporting during development and document a specific budget (a reasonable target to validate against, not to assume: 100–150k CU with headroom) rather than relying on defaults blindly.
- **No unbounded, attacker-influenced-length loop anywhere.** The only caller-influenced variable affecting instruction cost is the payout table outcome count supplied at `open_ticket`, and it's bounded by `MAX_OUTCOMES` at the point it's *written* — so any later loop that reads it back is provably bounded too. State this as an explicit invariant an auditor can check for by grepping every loop bound back to a compile-time constant.
- **`remaining_accounts`.** None of Fade's specified instructions obviously need it — explicitly reject a non-empty `remaining_accounts` list unless a specific, later feature justifies it. An unbounded, accepted `remaining_accounts` list is a classic Solana CU-griefing and account-substitution surface for no current benefit.

## §8 — Known Solana exploit patterns — is Fade exposed, and what specifically prevents it

| Exploit | Root cause | Fade exposed to the same class? | What specifically prevents it in Fade |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cashio** (Mar 2022, ~$52.8M) — [Cashio hack post-mortem](https://thinkanddev.com/cashio-hack-post-mortem), [Quadrigainitiative case study](https://www.quadrigainitiative.com/casestudy/cashioappsolanafakeaccountexploit.php) | No root-of-trust for a chain of account dependencies — a forged, same-shaped "collateral bank" account was accepted because its *fields* looked right, not because its *provenance* was checked. | **Yes, structurally** — every "is this really the vault's vault / really this ticket's beneficiary / really integrator X's authority" check in Fade is the identical question Cashio got wrong. | Every account of consequence is pinned via `address =`/`seeds =`/`has_one =` against already-trusted state (§1), never accepted on type-correctness alone. |
| **Wormhole** (Feb 2022, ~$320M) — [Halborn: Explained — The Wormhole Hack](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-wormhole-hack-february-2022), [CertiK: Wormhole Bridge Exploit Analysis](https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/wormhole-bridge-exploit-incident-analysis) | A fake account was substituted for the real Instructions sysvar; the signature-verification routine trusted the sysvar's *content* without checking the account's own *address* was the well-known sysvar constant. | **Directly relevant** — Fade plans real instruction-sysvar usage for the settle/deposit/withdraw composition guard (§5) and reasoned explicitly about sysvar limitations for CPI authentication (§2). | Any sysvar account Fade reads is address-checked against the well-known sysvar id (`Sysvar<'info, Instructions>` in Anchor, or an explicit `id()` check) before its contents are trusted — never accepted as a bare positional `AccountInfo`. |
| **Mango Markets** (Oct 2022, ~$116M) — [Ackee Blockchain: 2022 Solana Hacks Explained — Mango Markets](https://ackee.xyz/blog/2022-solana-hacks-explained-mango-markets/) | A thinly-traded market's oracle price was self-traded to an extreme, and used uncapped for collateral valuation, enabling a massively over-collateralized-looking loan. | **Low direct exposure** — Fade has no volatile, thinly-traded price oracle; it holds a single stablecoin and prices shares off internal deposited/withdrawn/settled amounts, not a market price. | Keep it that way: never let any future feature price payout table outcomes, exposure limits, or liability off a live, manipulable on-chain price/market feed. If ever introduced, apply the same discipline (deviation caps, TWAP, circuit breakers) Mango lacked. |
| **Loopscale** (Apr 2025, ~$5.8M) — [Halborn: Explained — The Loopscale Hack](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-loopscale-hack-april-2025) | A manipulable exotic-collateral pricing function let undercollateralized loans appear sufficiently collateralized, enabling over-withdrawal against the vault. | **Directly analogous** — the equivalent Fade bug is `open_ticket` reserving more liability than the vault can actually cover. | The gross-solvency invariant `total_reserved_liability + open_vault_credits ≤ total_assets`, and `κ`, are checked and enforced **atomically, inside `open_ticket`**, off the internal ledger, with a hard abort on insufficient headroom (§3) — no TOCTOU window, no "best effort" partial reservation. |
| **marginfi flash-loan bypass** (Solana-native lending, 2024) — [Asymmetric Research: Threat Contained — marginfi Flash Loan Vulnerability](https://blog.asymmetric.re/threat-contained-marginfi-flash-loan-vulnerability/) | A later-added instruction (`transfer_to_new_account`) let liabilities be moved to a fresh account mid-flash-loan, bypassing a repayment check that only inspected the *original* account. | **Directly relevant as a process risk**, not just a code risk — any *future* instruction touching `vault`, `integrator_config`, `ticket`, or a request account could reintroduce exactly this bug if it doesn't preserve every existing invariant. | Treat this as a standing rule (§9): every future PR touching those account types is re-checked against the full §1/§3/§4 invariant list, not reviewed in isolation. |
| **ERC-4626 first-depositor / donation inflation attack** (cross-chain-general, directly applicable) — [OpenZeppelin: A Novel Defense Against ERC4626 Inflation Attacks](https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/a-novel-defense-against-erc4626-inflation-attacks) | Live-balance-derived NAV lets a raw token donation manipulate share price for a first depositor's benefit. | **Yes, fully exposed by default** — any share-based vault with a balance-derived NAV has this exact bug. | Internal-ledger NAV, never live-balance-derived (§3, §6) — the fix is structural, not incidental. |
| **Predictable/weak on-chain RNG in gambling contracts** (general, well-documented pattern across chains for naive dice/coinflip-style contracts relying on block/slot-derived pseudo-randomness) — [Adevar Labs: On-Chain Randomness on Solana — Predictability, Manipulation & Safer Alternatives](https://www.adevarlabs.com/blog/on-chain-randomness-on-solana-predictability-manipulation-safer-alternatives-part-1) | Randomness derived from `Clock`/`SlotHashes`/recent blockhash is deterministic and can be predicted or influenced by whoever controls transaction content, ordering, or the leader slot. | **Zero exposure, by design** — as long as the rule below holds. | Randomness comes from ORAO VRF, a **push**/quorum design whose fulfilment the quorum writes unconditionally (not a pull/commit-reveal design like Switchboard, which is not integrated in V1) — never a block/slot-derived value used as the outcome's sole entropy, even as a "temporary" or "testnet-only" fallback. `SlotHashes` is never the sole entropy: the adopted binding (Technical Specification §3.6) deliberately derives the on-chain ORAO request seed from a `SlotHashes` entry that did not exist when the ticket opened, but the outcome itself is always selected by the fulfilled ORAO result, not by that slot hash alone. **Audit action: grep the entire outcome-selection code path for any `Clock::get()` or `SlotHashes` usage that by itself determines the outcome, and treat any hit as a finding** — the seed-derivation use of `SlotHashes` in `request_randomness` is not that; it is bound into a seed whose outcome the key holders cannot know until ORAO's quorum fulfils it. |

## §9 — Audit and testing plan

### Stateful property tests and fuzzing

- Full instruction-sequence fuzzing across `request_deposit → strike_epoch → claim_deposit → open_ticket → request_randomness → settle_ticket → request_withdraw → claim_withdraw` in randomized order — with fresh authorities whose first `open_ticket` creates their record — randomized valid/invalid account substitutions, and randomized payout table shapes, using `proptest-state-machine` driving LiteSVM against a reference model. Trident is not used: it targets solana-sdk 2.3 and cannot build against Anchor 1.2 (Technical Specification §2).
- Custom invariants asserted after **every** flow, not just at the end of a run:
    - `total_reserved_liability + open_vault_credits <= total_assets` and `total_reserved_liability <= 0.8 × total_assets` — always.
    - `shares_mint.supply` equals the sum of every LP share balance — always.
    - Injecting a deliberate raw donation to the vault token account never changes `total_assets` or any strike's share price.
    - Every non-`Settled`/non-`Expired` ticket contributes its reserved liability to `total_reserved_liability` exactly once — no double-count on open, no double-release on settle or expiry.
    - `shares_mint.supply == 0` if and only if `total_assets == 0` (no orphaned value with no claimants, no claimants against zero value).
    - A strike never runs while a ticket opened before its close is still open (invariant 16), and the two epoch counters and their generation return to the values the open/close sequence implies — no leak, no double-decrement.
    - Every closed account's rent lamports arrived at the address recorded as its `rent_payer`/`owner`, never at the transaction signer, across all closable account types.
- Targeted `cargo-fuzz` runs on the payout-table validation and outcome-selection functions of `fade-math`, in isolation, with boundary inputs: `u64::MAX` and `0` price, 1-outcome and `MAX_OUTCOMES`-outcome payout tables, weight sums at the `u64`/`u128` boundary — to catch the overflow/precision classes from §3 directly rather than waiting for a full instruction-sequence run to stumble onto them.

### LiteSVM (deterministic integration tests)

- **One test per checklist row in §1** — the account-validation table in the parent is written specifically so it doubles as the test plan's table of contents: wrong-mint token account → `request_deposit` rejected; unrelated PDA as `integrator_authority` → `open_ticket` rejected; wrong `beneficiary` → `settle_ticket` rejected; wrong `randomness_account` → `settle_ticket` rejected; etc.
- The composition-guard tests from §5: construct a single transaction containing `settle_ticket` bundled with `request_deposit`/`request_withdraw`, using LiteSVM's ability to build precise multi-instruction transactions, and assert rejection.
- The revival-attack tests from §4: attempt to resurrect a closed `ticket` or a claimed request account by re-funding the address and reusing it, and confirm rejection.
- The donation-attack test from §6: send a raw SPL transfer directly to the vault token account, then confirm the next strike's share price is unaffected.
- Rent-recipient tests: confirm every `close` constraint pays the account's recorded original payer, never the transaction's signer, across every closable account type.
- VRF-integration tests against mocked ORAO accounts (real oracle fulfillment can't run inside LiteSVM): feed `settle_ticket` a *pending*, a *wrong-owner/malformed*, and a *fulfilled* randomness account and confirm each is handled correctly — pending reverts cleanly with no state mutated, wrong-owner is rejected outright, and fulfilled produces the exact outcome a golden, pinned randomness value should select (deterministic golden-vector tests catch off-by-one and modulo-bias regressions that a purely randomized fuzz run might not reliably hit).

### Where external audit budget is genuinely worth spending

1. **The integrator-identity boundary — `open_ticket`'s account validation and the record it creates on a first call (§1, §2).** This is the newest, least battle-tested piece of Fade's design (most Solana programs don't need to attribute state to an arbitrary, never-registered *caller* the way Fade does) and sits in exactly the bug class that produced the largest historical Solana losses (Cashio, Wormhole — §8). Recommend a dedicated, narrowly-scoped review of just these account constraints — `open_ticket`'s identity and record creation, and `set_share_account` — independent of and before the full-program audit.
2. **The share/liability accounting invariants across the full `request_deposit → strike_epoch → open_ticket → settle_ticket → request_withdraw → claim_withdraw` lifecycle (§3, §4, §6).** This is where Cashio-adjacent root-of-trust bugs, ERC-4626-style rounding/inflation bugs, and Loopscale-style reservation-bypass bugs all converge. Hand the auditor the invariant list from the property-testing plan above as a starting brief and ask them to try to break each one specifically — invariant-driven review catches materially more real bugs for this class of program than open-ended line-by-line reading (this mirrors what Sec3's review did for Loopscale post-incident).
3. **The permissionless `settle_ticket` composition/atomicity surface, specifically the JIT deposit/withdraw-around-a-known-outcome defense (§5).** This is a genuinely novel-to-this-design economic/MEV surface, not a classic memory-safety bug, and benefits from a reviewer who thinks like a searcher rather than a line-by-line Rust auditor. Consider sourcing this specific review from someone with Solana MEV/searcher experience (e.g., Jito-searcher background) in addition to, not instead of, a traditional Anchor/Rust audit.

### Also worth doing, lower cost

- `cargo audit`/OSV scanning on the full Anchor/Solana crate dependency tree, with exact-pinned (not floating semver-range) versions for `anchor-lang` and `anchor-spl` (Fade links no ORAO crate: its ORAO account layouts are hand-written and checked against a vendored copy of ORAO's IDL), given how frequently subtle sysvar- and CPI-behavior-affecting changes have shipped across Solana/Agave versions historically.
- Re-run the full §1/§3/§4 invariant checklist against every *future* PR that touches `vault`, `queue`, `integrator`, `ticket`, or the request and epoch accounts — not as a one-time launch gate, but as a standing review rule (this is the direct lesson from the marginfi flash-loan bypass in §8: the bug was introduced by a later, seemingly-unrelated instruction, not the original design).
